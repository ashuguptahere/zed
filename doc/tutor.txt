     Welcome to the zedit tutor!  zedit is a modal editor: in Normal mode the
     keys are commands, not text.  This file is a scratch copy: edit it
     freely.  Practice each command right here.

     Work through the lessons in order; each one builds on the last.
     Remember: learning comes from doing, not from reading.  Do the
     exercises.  If you ever get stuck in a strange state, press Esc a
     few times to get back to Normal mode.

     Lines starting with ---> are practice lines meant to be changed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                         Lesson 1: MOVING THE CURSOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  In Normal mode the cursor is moved with the h, j, k and l keys:

            k              Hint: h is on the left and moves left,
        h       l                l is on the right and moves right,
            j                    j points down like a hook.

  1. Move the cursor around the screen until you feel comfortable.
     (The arrow keys work too, but h j k l keep your hand on the row.)

  2. Word motions: press  w  to jump to the start of the next word,
     b  to jump back a word, and  e  to jump to the end of a word.

  3. Line motions:  0  jumps to the first column,  $  to the line's end.

  4. File motions:  gg  jumps to the first line of the file,  G  to the
     last line, and a count makes it absolute:  1G  or  gg  is line one.

  5. Counts multiply any motion: type  3w  to move three words at once,
     or  5j  to go five lines down.

---> Practice here: hop between the words of this line with w, b and e,
---> then bounce between 0 and $ a few times, then try 3w and 2b.

  Try  gg  now, then come back with  G  or a count like  20G.

                          Lesson 2: INSERTING TEXT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  To type text you enter Insert mode.  Esc always returns to Normal mode.

     i   insert before the cursor          a   append after the cursor
     o   open a new line BELOW and insert  O   open a new line ABOVE

  1. Move onto the practice line below, position the cursor where text
     is missing, press  i , type the missing letters, then press Esc.

---> Some text is miing from ths line.  Fix it with i.

  2. Now use  a  to append: put the cursor on the last letter of the
     short word and press  a  to add what is missing after it.

---> This sentence stops too soo

  3. Press  o  on the line below to open a new line under it and type a
     line of your own.  Then press Esc, and try  O  to open a line above.

---> Open a new line below this one with o, and above it with O.

  NOTE: the statusline shows the current mode.  When in doubt, Esc.

                      Lesson 3: DELETING AND CHANGING

  Deletion commands work in Normal mode:

     x     delete the character under the cursor
     dw    delete from the cursor to the start of the next word
     dd    delete the whole line
     d$    delete from the cursor to the end of the line
     cw    change a word: deletes it and drops you into Insert mode

     p     paste after the cursor / below the current line
     P     paste before / above
     v     Visual mode: select by character
     V     Visual mode: select whole lines

     paste a copy under it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                            Lesson 5: SEARCHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     /text   search forward for text        n   jump to the next match
     ?text   search backward                N   jump to the previous one
     *       search for the word under the cursor

  Searches are LITERAL, not regular expressions: /a.c matches the three
  characters "a.c", nothing else.  Search is incremental (the view jumps
  as you type; Esc cancels and puts the cursor back) and wraps around
  the end of the file.  Matches are highlighted.

  1. Type  /wombat  and press Enter.  Then press  n  a few times to
     visit every wombat in this lesson, and  N  to walk backwards.

---> A wild wombat appears on this line.
---> No marsupials here, keep going.
---> Two wombat sightings on one line: wombat.

  2. Put the cursor on the word "wombat" above and press  *  to search
     for it directly, without typing it.

  3. The same misspelling appears three times below.  Search for it with
     /teh  and fix each match with  cw  (remember  n  to hop along and
     .  to repeat the change).

---> Somewhere in teh middle of this line.
---> And again in teh middle of this one.
---> One last teh to hunt down and fix.

                Lesson 6: TEXT OBJECTS, SURROUND AND COMMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Text objects let an operator act on the thing AROUND the cursor, so
  you don't have to move to its start first:

     ciw    change the whole word under the cursor
     di"    delete everything inside the surrounding double quotes
     da(    delete a parenthesised group, parentheses included

  1. Put the cursor ANYWHERE inside the wrong word and press  ciw :

---> The quick brown elephant jumps over the lazy dog.

  2. Put the cursor between the quotes below and press  di" , then use
     i  to type a better message:

---> error: "this message is wrong and misleading"

  Surround commands add, change and delete the pair around something:

     ysiw)   surround the word under the cursor with ( )
     cs"'    change surrounding "double" quotes to 'single' quotes
     ds"     delete the surrounding quotes entirely

  3. Put the cursor on the word marked below and press  ysiw) :

---> The result of the call is retval and nobody can tell.

  4. Change the quoting style of this string with  cs"' :

---> name = "double quoted, but style demands single quotes"

  5. Strip the needless quotes below with  ds" :

---> count = "42"

  COMMENTS:  gcc  toggles the comment state of the current line, using
  the comment syntax of the file's language.  Press  gcc  twice on the
  line below and watch it toggle (this file has no language, so try it
  later in a source file; gc with a motion comments a whole range).

---> const answer = 42;

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                        Lesson 7: FILES AND WINDOWS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Commands starting with  :  are typed on the command line; Enter runs
  them and Esc cancels.

     :w          write (save) the file        :q    quit
     :wq         write and quit               :q!   quit without saving
     :e file     open a file in this window

  1. Type  :w  now to save your tutor progress.  (This copy is yours —
     saving is safe.)

  Several files can be open at once, each keeping its own cursor and
  undo history:

     :ls         list the open buffers
     :bn / :bp   switch to the next / previous buffer
     ]b / [b     the same, as normal-mode keys (2]b skips one)
     :bd         close the current buffer

  Windows split the screen so two buffers (or two views of one) are
  visible at the same time:

     :split  or  :vsplit    split horizontally / vertically
     Ctrl-w w                jump to the next window
     Ctrl-w c                close the current window

  2. Try it: type  :vsplit , press  Ctrl-w w  a couple of times to hop
     between the windows, then close one with  Ctrl-w c .

  3. Open another file with  :e <path> , look around, then come back to
     the tutor with  :bp  (or  :bn ), and check  :ls  along the way.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                         Lesson 8: PICKERS AND LSP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The Space key is the leader: press  Space  and a which-key popup lists
  what can follow.  In any picker: type to filter, move with Ctrl-n and
  Ctrl-p (or the arrows), open with Enter, cancel with Esc.

     Space f f  fuzzy file finder (matches file NAMES)
     Space f w  project-wide content search (greps file CONTENTS;
                regex, same syntax as /)
     Space f b  switch between the open buffers
     Space b    Buffers menu: picker, next/previous, close
     Space e    file explorer: a tree of this directory on the side

  Every picker looks the same: the explorer on one side (when open), the
  results in the middle, and a preview of the highlighted file on the
  right.  Ctrl-d and Ctrl-u — or the mouse wheel — scroll that preview
  without moving your selection.

  1. Press  Space f f , type a few letters of a file name, watch the
     preview follow your selection, then press Esc to cancel (or Enter
     to open it — remember :bp brings you back).

  2. Press  Space f w  and search the project for the word  wombat .
     This very file should show up.

  3. Press  Space e  for the file tree: j/k move, Enter opens, h
     collapses, q closes.  Space still opens the leader menu in there.
     While the tree is open it follows you: switching buffers expands
     and selects the active file's row.

  The title bar across the top shows one tab per open buffer (the
  active one highlighted, a dot marking unsaved ones) next to the
  explorer's header; clicking a tab switches to it.

  When a language server is installed for the file type (zls, clangd,
  pylsp, typescript-language-server), zedit starts it automatically:

     gd         go to the definition of the symbol under the cursor
     K          show hover documentation for it
     gr         rename the symbol (prompts on the command line; edits in
                other files are applied too — :wa saves all buffers)
     gi / gy    go to the implementation / the type definition
     Space l R  list every reference to the symbol in a picker
     Space l s  document symbols — jump to a function or type here
     Space l S  workspace symbols — search them across the whole
                project, including files you have never opened
     Space l D  every diagnostic in every open buffer, in one list
     Space l f  format the document (:w also formats when the config's
                format_on_save is true, the default)
     ga         list code actions for the current line in a picker
     ]d / [d    jump to the next / previous diagnostic line

  Problems the server reports appear three ways at once: a coloured sign
  in the gutter, the message inline at the end of the offending line, and
  the full text in the statusline when the cursor is on that line.  Put
  inline_diagnostics = false in your config for a quieter buffer.

  Started with no file at all, zedit greets you with the files and
  directories you opened recently: j/k to select, Enter to open, or
  press 1-9 to jump straight to one.

  Files on another machine work too, with no setup on that machine:

     zedit ssh://server/etc/hosts        from the shell
     :e ssh://server/var/log/app.log     from inside the editor
     :ssh server/srv/www                 browse a remote directory

  :w writes back over the same connection.

  Watch the right of the statusline while you type a command: it shows
  what you have entered so far ( d , then di , then diw runs and it
  clears), so a half-finished command is never a mystery.

  In a source file with a supported language, whole constructs are text
  objects:  daf  deletes the function around the cursor,  dif  just its
  body,  dac  a class or struct,  daa  an argument (with its comma) and
  dia  just the argument,  daC  a comment;  ]f  /  [f  jump between
  functions.

  Paragraphs are text objects too:  }  and  {  jump to the next and
  previous blank line, and  dip  /  dap  delete the paragraph you are
  in (dap also takes the blank line after it).  Try  }  a few times in
  this file, then  Ctrl-o  to come back.

  In Insert mode the completion popup appears on its own when you pause
  while typing a word; Ctrl-n requests it immediately.  Ctrl-n and
  Ctrl-p (or the arrows) move through it, Tab or Enter accepts, Esc
  dismisses.  Matching is fuzzy, so typing  mplt  finds  mockComplete .
  Set  auto_completion = false  in your config for manual-only popups.

  Some completions are snippets.  Accepting one fills in placeholder
  text and drops the cursor on the first placeholder: just type to
  replace it, press Tab to move to the next, and Esc when you are done.

  The command line completes too: type  :e   and press Tab to cycle
  through the files here (Shift-Tab goes backward), or  :the<Tab>  to
  get  :theme .  Up and Down recall earlier commands that start with
  what you have typed — try  :  then Up.

  And it suggests as you type, like the fish shell: the rest of the
  newest matching earlier command (or a command name) appears as dim
  ghost text after the cursor.  Press Right (or End) to accept it;
  Enter always runs only what you actually typed, so the ghost can
  never surprise you.

  This tutor file has no language server, so try these keys later in a
  real source file — diagnostics appear as gutter signs on their own.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                            Lesson 9: GOING FURTHER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  You now know enough to do real work.  A few directions to grow next,
  all covered by the full documentation:

   - Precision motions:  f F t T  jump to a character in the line
     (repeat with ; and ,), and  %  bounces between matching brackets.
   - Marks:  ma  remembers a spot,  `a  jumps back to it.
   - Macros:  qa ... q  records your keystrokes,  @a  replays them.
   - Registers:  "ayy  yanks into register a,  "ap  pastes from it.
   - Multiple cursors:  Ctrl-n / Ctrl-p  add a caret on the line below
     or above; typing edits every line at once; Esc collapses them.
   - Scrolling:  Ctrl-d / Ctrl-u  move by half a page, and the mouse
     wheel scrolls three lines at a time.
   - Undo is a tree:  u  and  Ctrl-r  step back and forward as usual, but
     if you undo and then type something new, the old version is not
     lost.   g-  and  g+  walk every state in the order you made them,
     :earlier 10s  goes back in time,  :earlier 1f  goes back to your
     last save, and  :undolist  shows them all in a picker so you can
     jump straight to one.  Put  persistent_undo = true  in your config
     and the history is still there tomorrow.
   - Long lines wrap onto the next screen row at a space, not mid-word,
     marked with a small arrow in the gutter, and keep their indent so
     the wrapped part hangs under the line it belongs to.  j and k still step whole lines, as in vim;  gj  and
     gk  step one screen row, and  g0 / g$  reach the ends of one.  Put
     soft_wrap = false  in your config to scroll sideways instead.
   - Themes:  :theme  or  Space f t  switches between the five built-in
     palettes;  zedit --init-config  writes a commented config file.
   - Staying current:  :update  asks whether a newer zedit was released
     (it is the only thing that ever touches the network, and only when
     you ask).

  For the complete reference:

   - the man page:  man zedit  (or  man ./doc/zedit.1  from the repo),
   - CLAUDE.md in the repository root: the full, honest list of every
     motion, operator, text object, picker and LSP feature.
